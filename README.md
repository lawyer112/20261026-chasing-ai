# 当 AI 替代了所有工作，人类的工作就剩下催 AI 吗？

When AI Takes Over Every Job, Will Our Job Be to Chase the AI?

AI 写完了报告、画好了海报、做好了页面，人为什么还不能下班？这篇文章从三个 AI 使用不同促销折扣的交付事故出发，讨论自动化之后留下的协调、验收、待命，以及“证明自己还在工作”的要求。

## 论文简介

“AI 把工作都做完了，人类就负责催 AI”看似是一个顺理成章的未来。本篇把这个判断拆开：**生成结果、满足交付条件、解除人的待命义务，是三件不同的事。** 机器可以很快完成第一件，组织却仍把后两件留给人。

文章沿着一个办公室的工作过程，讨论为什么催办有时是在解决交接问题，有时是在争抢有限资源，有时只是向考核系统证明自己在线。它进一步追问：如果连提醒、验收和跟进也能自动完成，留下来的岗位由谁付钱，人又是否有权支配省下的时间？

主要论点包括：

- **任务都完成，不代表项目已经交付。** 各个 AI 分别完成指令，仍可能使用不一致的要求。统一有效版本、验证成果是否可用，才是协调的实际价值。
- **操作减少，不代表自由时间增加。** 在允许合并检查且不遗漏紧急事件的假设下，同样一小时操作 12 分钟、空闲 48 分钟，最长连续空闲可以是 4 分钟，也可以是 48 分钟。
- **催得更勤，不一定提高总产出。** 当资源总量不变、排队规则偏向“最近被催”的任务时，催促可能只改变谁先得到资源，并把所有人卷入更多消息。
- **能管理 AI，不等于掌握工作的决定权。** 是否能拒绝无意义的任务、判断成果、支配节省的时间，比岗位名称里有没有“经理”更能说明人的处境。

文章用假想办公室、条件推理和可复算的排班比较展开，并结合自动化与人机协作研究。排班数字是设定下的计算，不是劳动者调查；其中的结论用于辨认不同工作安排，没有给出未来就业人数的预测。完整论证、图表和参考文献均在中英文 PDF 中。

## English

### About the paper

AI has written the report, drawn the poster, and built the page. Why can the human still not leave work? Beginning with an imagined delivery failure in which three AIs use different promotional discounts, this essay examines coordination, acceptance checks, availability, and the obligation to demonstrate continued activity.

**[Read the complete English PDF](en.pdf) · [Chinese PDF](zh.pdf) · [Discussion and feedback](https://github.com/lawyer112/20261026-chasing-ai/issues)**

Author: lawyer112 · Paper ID: 20261026-chasing-ai · Content version: 0.2.1 · Published: 2026-10-27

The premise that humans will make a living chasing AI overlooks three distinct outcomes: **producing an output, meeting delivery conditions, and releasing a person from the obligation to remain available.** Automating the first does not automatically achieve the other two.

Following an office workflow, the essay asks when follow-up work repairs handoffs, competes for scarce resources, or merely demonstrates attendance. If reminders, acceptance checks, and follow-up can themselves be automated, who pays for the remaining role, and who controls the time saved?

Its main arguments are:

- **Completed tasks do not guarantee a delivered project.** Individual AIs can satisfy incompatible instructions. Establishing the valid requirements and verifying a usable result gives coordination its practical value.
- **Less activity does not guarantee more free time.** If checks can be batched without missing urgent events, the same 12 active and 48 idle minutes in an hour can leave a longest uninterrupted gap of either 4 or 48 minutes.
- **More reminders need not increase total output.** With fixed capacity and priority for the most recently chased task, reminders may only redistribute access while drawing everyone into more messaging.
- **Managing AI does not necessarily confer authority over work.** The ability to refuse pointless tasks, assess results, and control time saved says more about a job than a managerial title.

The argument combines an imagined office, conditional reasoning, a reproducible schedule comparison, and research on automation and human-machine interaction. The schedule figures are calculations under stated assumptions, not survey results, and the paper does not forecast employment totals. Both PDFs contain the complete argument, figures, and references.
